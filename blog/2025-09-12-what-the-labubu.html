what the Labubu

Originally published: September 12, 2025

I saw the girl behind me in my class take a sip from her owala water bottle, and in this, I saw a seemingly brand new “Labubu”, attached as a keychain on the handle. I was inquisitive about it because they just came on my radar a couple of weeks ago, and I gestured to ask her about it. she however had air pods in and didn’t see or hear me. so I guess I will keep wondering.

I don’t exactly know or care why the Labubu is such a thing, but this is no new tale. seeing this made me think a bit about the items that we prescribe identities to, and how that fits into performance vs authenticity (for the internet and social groups) and also sub-cultures, or the lack thereof of them.

another seemingly popular internet trend/meme lately would be the stereotype of the “performative male”. this man notably dons a cutesy tote bag, a sealed Clairo vinyl, a book either about women, or written by a woman, a matcha latte, perhaps some rings, unoffensive streetwear, and quite possibly, a labubu. I don’t exactly know if this version of male actually exists in the wild, and most people are miles deeper than the stereotypes that are prescribed to them by the collective conscience.

this begs the question, is someone who gets on trend with labubu and owala or whatever the current “core” is on TikTok, grabbing onto these things for identity, or is it because they like the expressionism? or is there an air of validation when one posts or shows these things off? probably a mix of all and person dependent.

I do not think less of someone for participating in trends or whatnot, do what makes you happy, and I have participated or purchased products because the internet made them popular overnight. this is a consequence of being online in 2025 and having disposable income.

however, the bottom line with many of the biggest trends is that they oftentimes cost money, and are commodified or gamified via capitalism. to my understanding, the labubu is the epitome of this in that there is a rarity to them, or sort of a lottery system, to incentivize collecting them and purchasing more. plushie gambling… how interesting

I am of the belief that as soon as “capitalism” knows you exist, you will be targeted and marketed to in some capacity. capitalism has sort of commodified or made it harder to be authentic.

so, subcultures tie into this, because, is a subculture a trend? or is it what we could call more authentic? I did not live before the internet existing, but I can speculate that many people that didn’t align with the core of what “most people do” likely created special / safe spaces to express themselves, i.e. subcultures. whether this pertains to gender/sexuality expression, music, arts, politics, or anything else, I think these things were niche and less accessible. they also were rarely being broadcast to the entire world

so fast forward to 2025, and the onset of TikTok / reels and algos serving ads or what they deem as the best fit, absolutely molds the collective conscience and I would argue has and will almost assimilate subcultures entirely. at least in the sense they used to exist in, maybe? (could there be a downstream effect of radicalization because of this, probably?)

I have seen people online describe this as the “culture being flattened”, in that anything and everything can be seen at mass scale, instantly, so the net effect is that everything and nothing is truly niche, and I would describe it as a sort of memetic blender. yes, there are still trends, memes, and artists or whatnot that transcend the internet and TikTok, but everything is less collectivized. when the media/medium is so vastly different and speedier than, say something like getting information thru a newspaper, or radio, the turnover is very fast.

I think perhaps, this is where the entire phenomenon of people that trend-hop and fall into whatever is hot at the current moment, spawns from. I don’t think there will ever be a grunge music subculture that explodes into the mainstream like it was in the 90s, or art collectives at scale doing what they do best. I think we yearn for them, but the landscape is different.

I don’t necessarily know if this is all good, or bad, it is just where humanity and the speed of information is at now. everything is accessible and you don’t have to be years invested into a subculture, to get the outward look/feel of it. think perhaps of the emo movement, the world regarded them as freaks but you rarely saw a “casual” emo early in that movement, at least I think. now, every other person can wear a all black outfit and would call themselves “emo for the day”.

the barrier to entry is very low for almost everything, which is awesome but also strange. like I said above, this flattens everything…?

so then is a “poser” someone who just picks up onto the look / feel / item of a group or stereotype or how they want to be perceived, but is not genuinely that? a “performative male” is also sort of this, in that they don’t really care about the things they flaunt to achieve that look, it is to attract attention? perhaps a poser wants to belong to a subculture, for the sake of community, i.e. blending in, but doesn’t want to flaunt or attract. someone performing, is doing the latter.

and in this too, you have another large subset of people who don’t trend hop or prescribe to any sort of identity thru a subculture or anything. I suppose this is authenticity. someone who does as they please, for them, regardless of what it may be. I fear that what present forms we have of subcultures, may be more of a performance / upholding an image, vs true expressionism and authenticity. (because of the internet)

I am not incredibly sure if much of this made sense, but post!
