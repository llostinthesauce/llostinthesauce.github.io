gap

Originally published: September 30, 2025

strikingly, I have grown fond of the idea about a gap between all things. I mean this as, the semantics and language we use to describe items or feelings in life, will NEVER achieve the root sensation or goal. I also mean it in that goals are approximations. rarely does a plan ever go… to plan. even morals are what people aim for, but no one is perfect enough to not contradict themselves every now and again. it is what makes us human. we are not computers or calculators, immune to error.

taken a bit from some ideas on love, in a Greek sense, there is the loose idea that desire is always perpetual, and can never be achieved. Buddhist texts know this, as it is one of the four pillars of the practice. here, they point to desire as the root of all suffering, and suggest pure detachment and mindfulness as a resolve. while parts of me agree with this, (and it is pretty based in a purely religious sense), I fail to draw the same ultimate conclusion, at least right now. and i have NOT engaged heavily in these texts.

you could say that Buddhist practices are more relevant than ever, in the scope of modern society in 2025, which is littered with infinite dopamine slot machines and attention grabbers, freezing boredom and slowing down the speed of life. but a critic could also call this more detached to “modern society” than ever and dismiss it. Hmmm….

in generation z, we often chalk conflict or people into categories, pathologizing our emotions, concepts, and digging very “deep”, when perhaps it is not warranted. or perhaps I describe myself… One one hand, it is great that we all speak of our emotions with therapeutic language, something that perhaps we do better than our parents (and their parents,) but how many of us actually just feel what we try so hard to describe? alas, a gap is born. some ten year old out there is talking about trauma dumping and depression, but doesn’t know what that actually feels like to them. this isn’t inherently a bad thing, just interesting to me.

Lispector attempts to close her own gap in her novel, Agua Viva, and the prose is a breath of fresh air in these ideas. words are ultimately one tool in the box of expression, and are flawed in their own right. many things just are, many feelings will never be described, many events cannot be encapsulated. I prefer it this way.

the gap applies to many scopes of life, and I have found myself turning the idea over in my head. it can almost feel alienating and cold. if there is a gap between how I make myself known to others and how I am perceived, I truly am alone. or perhaps my words here, are doing my best to describe this gap, but I, will still fail to achieve it. it can never be achieved fully. I would argue that the “best” texts get mostly there, but a resonant truth or 1:1 explanation is impossible.

all of this to say that life, nature, time, and my own reality, is always thru my lens, as is everyone else’s thru theirs. we all communicate, express ourselves, and want to be seen, but there will always be a gap.

I think we cling tightly to those who decrease the distance of our personal gaps

and being is not becoming.
